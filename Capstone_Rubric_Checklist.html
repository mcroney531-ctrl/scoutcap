Rating System & Vocabulary

Core Terms Defined while talking through Synthesis logic
Talent Grade Decided
Production Agent's output. Independent of situation — purely "how good is this player." Hybrid scale: generated internally as a numeric score (0-100) with explicit calibration anchors in the prompt so the model actually uses the full range instead of clustering around 80. Converted to a letter grade (A-F, with +/-) for display in the final report.
  • Production efficiency — yards/touch, target share, TD rate, completion % (position-dependent)
  • Level of competition — conference strength, quality of opponents faced, not just raw stat totals
  • Breakout age — how young they were when they became the clear top option on their own team; younger breakouts are more predictive than the same stats produced later
  • Recruiting pedigree — ESPN recruiting rank/star rating as a long-range talent signal, independent of college production
  • Athletic measurables — combine numbers if available, though not every prospect gets invited or tested
Opportunity Grade Decided
Situation Agent's output. Independent of talent — purely "how good is the landing spot/depth chart." Same hybrid approach as Talent Grade — numeric (0-100) internally, letter grade for display.
  • Draft capital — round/pick, as a proxy for organizational investment and expectation
  • Current depth chart position — Sleeper's live data
  • Positional competition — entrenched veteran ahead of them, or genuinely open competition
  • Offensive scheme/volume — pass-heavy vs. run-heavy team, historical target/touch volume tendencies
  • Coaching staff track record — history of playing/developing rookies at this position early
Risk Modifier Decided
Output shape: a 1-5 durability score plus a chance-of-injury percentage — borrowing the presentation concept from Draft Sharks' injury guide, but computed internally from ESPN's athletes/{id}/injuries and Sleeper's live status fields rather than fetched from Draft Sharks directly. Why not fetch from Draft Sharks: their per-player injury pages have a soft usage cap (resets in incognito, suggesting client-side cookie tracking) — fine for casual browsing, but an agent evaluating a full 30+ prospect draft class in one session would hit that limit almost immediately, and building a tool that resets sessions to dodge it would mean circumventing a deliberate monetization mechanism rather than just using free public content. ESPN/Sleeper have no such cap.
  • Injury frequency — number of distinct incidents, not just total games missed
  • Severity — soft tissue (lower concern) vs. structural/surgical (higher concern)
  • Recency — a recent injury weighs differently than something from two years ago
  • Position-specific baseline risk — some positions carry higher inherent injury rates regardless of individual history
Exact formula combining these four criteria into the 1-5 score is still part of the broader Composite Logic weighting work below — shape and source are locked, internal math isn't yet.
Crowd Sentiment Signal Decided
Derived from Sleeper's trending add/drop data. Resolved as relative ranking, not absolute threshold: rookies are ranked against each other's activity levels at the same point in time, not against a fixed "high vs. low" cutoff. A player absent from the trending list isn't treated as inherently low-interest — overall platform activity is seasonal (e.g. a typical offseason lull means uniformly low volume across the board), so absence can just reflect timing, not disinterest. Standing out during a quiet period is actually a stronger signal than standing out during a high-volume spike (e.g. right after the actual NFL draft).
  • Rank within the current draft class — relative to other rookies being evaluated right now, not an absolute count threshold
  • Net direction — trending up vs. down over the recent window, not just a snapshot
  • Position-relative context — what's "high" for a TE is different from what's "high" for a WR
  • Seasonal awareness caveat — agent should note when overall platform activity is low league-wide, so sentiment reads as directional color rather than a decisive factor
Composite Score / Recommendation Logic Base weighting decided
The rule Synthesis uses to turn Talent + Opportunity + Risk + Sentiment into a round.pick recommendation. Base weighting locked: Talent 43% / Opportunity 38% / Risk 15% / Sentiment 4%. Market Consensus (KTC) deliberately excluded from this formula — stays a comparison-only step, not an input (see below). Risk and Sentiment kept intentionally small as modifiers rather than primary drivers; the freed-up weight from removing Market Consensus went to Talent and Opportunity proportionally, preserving that intent.
  • Position-specific weighting — Decided: stays uniform. Same Talent 43 / Opportunity 38 / Risk 15 / Sentiment 4 weighting applies across all positions, no per-position flexing. Simpler, defensible design choice.
Market Consensus Comparison (KeepTradeCut) Decided
A separate, final step — not an input to the Composite Logic above. Synthesis reaches its own conclusion first from independently-built reasoning, then checks it against KTC's crowdsourced dynasty value as a sanity check, explicitly surfacing agreement or the specific reason behind any divergence. Keeps the agent's reasoning genuinely independent rather than just echoing market consensus.
Open: does KTC have a documented public API, or does this require fetching/parsing the rendered page? Needs the same diligence as the ESPN endpoint research before building.

Floor / Ceiling range, not a single blended number Decided
Talent and Opportunity Grades are NOT collapsed into one score. Instead: Floor = pick if situation doesn't improve, Ceiling = pick if talent wins out, Recommended = the actual call between them. The tension between the two grades becomes the explanation for the range, not a problem to hide.
Updated shape: "Talent: A- / Opportunity: C+ → Floor: 4.02, Ceiling: 1.08 → Recommended: 2.11"
League format locked Decided
4 rounds, 12 picks per round — 48 total picks. Recommendation output uses round.pick notation (e.g. "4.04").
Pick-level precision vs. confidence-scaled range Decided
Resolved: the pick number is always a single, precise estimate — no range-widening based on confidence. By late June, even small-school prospects have real draft outcomes, write-ups, and spring practice reports, so thin-data cases are rare at this point in the calendar (mostly a pre-draft concern, not now). The exact pick functions as a relative positioning signal, not a literal "draft here" instruction — same way ADP gets used in practice.
